## Product priorities

- The team prioritizes frontend UI/UX and ideation for the competition. Preserve the core idea and improve it in iterations; prioritize a clear, convincing user journey over expanding backend or integration work.
- The challenge's central loop is to gather health indicators, help astronauts understand and evaluate them, and guide a useful next action. Assess proposed features against that loop.
- The ghost baseline and clickable body are GhostPilot's product choices for serving that loop. The challenge brief does not require those specific mechanisms.
- Do not assume users know what to measure, how to measure it, what a result means, or what to do next. Make those explanations concise and accessible in the relevant flow.
- The brief's example indicators and optional features are possibilities, not a requirement to implement every measurement or every suggestion.
- Distinguish current functionality from concepts and simulated demonstrations. Keep personal records, synthetic history, archived NASA data, avatar fitting, and illustrative body adaptations clearly identified.

The full supplied wording lives in `research/challenge-brief.md`; refer to it directly rather than reconstructing the challenge from an earlier summary or from the existing implementation.
